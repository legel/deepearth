## 1. The hypothesis that was wrong, and what it uncovered

`NEXT_STEPS.md` asked one question: is site3's burned creek channel *continuous* from the Gee
Creek pour point to a domain boundary? The answer is **yes** — 22.17 km inside the domain, two
components, both reaching an edge, stable across snap tolerances of 0.1 to 15 m.

A first pass suggested 16 disjoint components. That was an artefact of `shapely.linemerge`,
which splits at junctions, so a perfectly connected dendritic network reports as many pieces.
Proper node-graph connectivity was needed to answer the question at all.

Testing the hypothesis is what surfaced everything below.

## 2. Track B — thirteen defects in the pipeline

**The solver destroyed its own DEM conditioning.** `flood_sim_ian.py` bilinear-downsampled the
conditioned DEM from 0.875 m to 5 m. richdem's breaching carves drainage one cell wide; any
averaging kernel blends that trench back into the surrounding ground.

| DEM | trapped depression storage |
|---|---|
| native 0.875 m conditioned | **0.000 Mm³** |
| 5 m bilinear — what the solver integrated | **3.710 Mm³**, 79.4 mm domain-average |

79.4 mm is 20 % of the Ian storm and almost exactly the entire observed runoff volume. The
logged "fill adds zero volume" was true *at native resolution*, which is not the surface the
solver reads. Fixed with `Resampling.min`. The identical bug existed in
`research/build_grid_surrogate_dataset_site3_crop.py`, a hand-rolled copy of the same function.

**site3's stream burn had never executed.** `HYDRO_GEO` was hardcoded to the main AOI, so site3
loaded the main AOI's six Shingle Creek flowlines — 34 km outside its own DEM. They rasterised
to an all-zero mask and the burn silently no-op'd: `dem_burned.tif` was bit-identical to the raw
DEM. `dem_hydro.py` now resolves paths through `site_registry` (`--site site3`) and raises on an
empty burn mask.

**A constant-depth burn does not route.** Once burned correctly, D8 still fragmented: largest
flow accumulation anywhere in the 46.78 km² domain was 0.99 km², and 191 of 399 sampled steps
along the main stem ran *uphill* downstream. A uniform carve lowers a channel but imposes no
direction. The burn now enforces monotonic descent using 3DHP's own digitised flow direction,
with depth scaled by stream order and the enforced gradient measured per order from the DEM.
Max accumulation 0.99 → 7.12 km²; pour-point catchment 0.14 → 3.72 km².

**The infiltration fix from the previous session reached only one of two sites.**
`soil/data/soil_storage.csv` existed for site3 but was never generated for the main AOI, so
every main-AOI number was produced with the unbounded infiltration that session had diagnosed
as a bug.

**`export_overlays.py` had been aborting since 2026-08-03.** It masked `hand.png` (512 px) with
a waterbody mask built at its own `SIZE` (2048 px). The `IndexError` fired partway through the
export chain, so everything after it was skipped and the hydro overlays were frozen for three
weeks.

Also fixed: pour-point snapping took the *nearest* stream cell rather than the largest channel
(0.14 km² catchment against 33.15 documented); the stream-initiation threshold was a raw cell
count, so one number meant 766 m² at site3 and 38,391 m² at the main AOI; `flow_dir.tif` was
documented but never written; the fully-conditioned DEM that D8 and HAND run on was never saved;
`src.nodata or -9999.0` silently replaced a legitimate nodata of 0.0; `reproject` without
declared nodata lets `Resampling.min` *fill* nodata holes with surrounding terrain; and a
hardcoded `area_capture_frac` in the viewer export went stale when the delineation changed.

Consequences at site3: stream cells 1,719,908 → 112,502 (2.82 % → 0.18 % of domain, against the
main AOI's 0.12 %); cells with HAND < 1 m 77.2 % → 17.2 %; `stream_network.geojson` 207 MB →
11.9 MB.

## 3. The solver's integration loop — four defects

Found by mass balance refusing to close, not by reading code.

| defect | effect | fix |
|---|---|---|
| physics integrated a CFL-limited `dt`; the clock advanced `dt_s` | a "72-hour" run delivered **7-11 %** of the storm | sub-step to each hyetograph interval |
| `cum_infil` charged the Horton *capacity* rate regardless of available rain | soil filled on paper without absorbing; infiltration 2.9 % of rain | charge `min(inf, P)·dt` |
| `CFL_ALPHA = 0.30` unstable at 5 m once water accumulates | **-517.8 %** residual, 8.99 m depths oscillating under zero rainfall | 0.15 |
| final-frame guard captured no end-of-run frame | broke any mass balance measured against `frames[-1]` | always land a frame on the end |

**The first two masked each other.** The broken clock meant `cum_infil` accrued over a fraction
of real time and never reached its cap. Fixing the clock is what made the accounting error bite.
The third was unreachable until the first was fixed, because the solver never accumulated enough
water to go unstable.

**A per-cell volume limiter was added and then reverted.** It was justified by a -12.6 % residual
that proved to be an artefact of the final-frame bug — outflow integrated to the end compared
against storage sampled 333 minutes earlier. Measured correctly the solver conserves mass to
-0.001 %, and the limiter changed results by 1 part in 10⁴. `mesh_shallow_water.py` genuinely
needs its equivalent; this solver does not.

## 4. Hypotheses tested and killed

Four, each by measurement rather than argument. Recorded so they are not re-derived:

- **Channel discontinuity.** The network is continuous (§1).
- **Conveyance capacity.** Along-channel bed slope on the solver grid is 1.94e-3, matching the
  natural 1.90e-3; at the existing n = 0.040 a channel depth of 0.14-0.62 m already yields
  0.30-0.80 m/s, the documented range for this stream class. Manning's *n* is not the bottleneck.
- **Measurement cross-section.** Gauge-cell discharge was added on the theory that domain-boundary
  outflow unfairly charges the traverse to the box edge. It reports a *later* response, not earlier.
- **Baseflow initial condition.** Pre-filling the channel to the depth its measured 45.2 cfs
  baseflow implies validates beautifully (47.9 cfs simulated at t=0) and then makes the run worse.
  Kept behind `--baseflow`, off by default. **This conclusion predates the solver fixes and needs
  re-testing.**

Track A reached the same verdict on roughness independently: a physically derived field spanning
9.2x moved runoff 8 % in the wrong direction.

## Open

1. **Ponded infiltration — the leading candidate for the remaining 2.3x overshoot.** Infiltration
   draws only from instantaneous rainfall, so once rain stops, ponded water sits on a half-empty
   profile and cannot enter it. **138 mm of the 206 mm cap is structurally unreachable.** Reaching
   it would move infiltration from 17.3 % to ~52 % and runoff from 72 % to ~37 %. A genuine
   missing process, but a modelling change rather than a bug fix — flagged for decision.
2. **Re-runs, all invalidated by the solver defects**: both probability ensembles, the storage-cap
   sweep, the `--baseflow` experiment, Track A's three-arm A/B.
3. **Flood extent at site3 has no ground truth at all** — no PlanetScope or Sentinel-2 coverage.
   Peak area moved 246 → 1,781 ha across this session with nothing to check it against.
4. Johns Lake is verified free of the resampling defect (its solver reads the DEM at native
   2.64 m with no downsampling step) but has not been re-checked against the others.
