FluxFill

Filling eddy-covariance tower records with a model of everything measured at every tower.

Mira Shivani Sankar, Lance Legel, Trevor Keenan. Quantitative Ecosystem Dynamics lab, University of California, Berkeley. Sponsored by Ecological Intelligence, Inc.

Correction, 30 October 2026. We found a leak in our scoring of site adaptation: weights adapted under one mask were reused for other masks of the same window, so they had seen values those masks hide. Every score of FluxFill with site adaptation is invalid until rescored, including the validation tables, the methane split, the adaptation figure, Fig. 1d and the Windows tab. Test scores of FluxFill without adaptation, Fig. 1a-c and Fig. 4 are not affected. The bug is fixed and the rescoring is running.

FluxFill is a self-supervised AI foundation model trained to reconstruct masked and missing flux data, based on the DeepEarth neural network architecture. Its input is a 180-day window of half-hourly data at one tower: the fluxes of carbon dioxide (NEE), latent heat (LE), sensible heat (H) and methane (FCH4), the tower's meteorology, reanalysis drivers, and Clay embeddings of Sentinel-2 images. In training, blocks of 1 to 180 days are hidden in any variable, including every variable at once, and the model predicts a distribution for each hidden value. The first application is filling gaps in flux tower records.

Filling a window

  1. Window180 days of half-hourly values for every variable at one tower. The tower's other years are available as context.
  2. Site adaptation300 fine-tuning steps on the window's visible values and the tower's other years. The weights are reset after the window.
  3. Samples64 samples of every hidden value from its predictive distribution. They give the fill and its 95% interval; sums over days and months are formed per sample.
  4. ScoresCRPS, 95% coverage, MAE, RMSE, MSE, bias, R² and WAPE on hidden measured values, for every method on the same windows and masks.

Results

CRPS is the main score of a probabilistic fill; lower is better. Each comparison uses only the hidden values that every method fills. Validation towers are used to select the model. Test towers are scored once for each final model; the current test scores are from the model before site adaptation. Full tables: Results.

    FluxFill-Bench

    A window is 180 days of one site at half-hourly resolution. Only measured values are ever scored. Test sites, held-out years and masks are fixed before any model is fitted. Specification: BENCHMARK.md.

    Regimes

    R1Gap inside a record at a test site: the window's unmasked observations are visible.
    R2Held-out final year at a training site.
    R3No tower data: every tower channel masked for the whole window; reanalysis and imagery remain.

    Mask families

    M1Distribution-matched gaps (Irvin et al. 2021), for continuity with published numbers.
    M2One contiguous block of 1, 7, 30, 90 or 180 days, uniform over the window, on all tower channels.
    M3A real joint outage of all tower channels transplanted from another site-year.
    M4One modality removed for the whole window: tower meteorology, reanalysis or Sentinel-2.

    Scores

    CRPS from joint samples (primary); CRPS and bias of daily, monthly and window sums; coverage of the 50%, 80% and 95% intervals and PIT histograms by gap length; MAE, MSE, RMSE, bias, R² and the weighted absolute percentage error of the predictive mean for comparison with the literature; correlation of daily anomalies and interannual variability; tail error.     Windows

    Each example is one 180-day window at a tower that FluxFill never trained on. A benchmark mask hides part of the window; every method receives the window with those values removed and fills them in. The hidden measurements are kept aside and used only to score the fill.

    Input: measured, visible to the method Hidden: removed by the mask Ground truth: the hidden measurement, used only for scoring Inference: predicted mean, 80% and 95% intervals
