Article

FluxFill: a masked generative flux foundation model fills eddy-covariance records across 2-hour to 180-day outages

Mira Shivani Sankar1, Lance Legel1,2, Trevor Keenan1

1Quantitative Ecosystem Dynamics lab, Department of Environmental Science, Policy, and Management, University of California, Berkeley. 2Ecological Intelligence, Inc.

Abstract

Eddy-covariance towers measure the exchange of carbon dioxide, water vapour, sensible heat and methane between ecosystems and the atmosphere every half-hour, and every record has gaps. In {{outage_tower_years}} tower-years at the training towers, station outages that remove every tower channel at once span {{outage_halfhours_m}} million half-hours, or {{outage_years}} years ({{outage_share}}% of the record), and {{gap_share_ge7d}}% of that time lies in outages of 7 days or longer. The methods in use fit one flux at one tower at a time from drivers measured by the same station, and their uncertainty has not been scored with a proper scoring rule on gaps of weeks to months. We present FluxFill, a self-supervised AI foundation model trained to reconstruct masked and missing flux data, based on the DeepEarth neural network architecture. It predicts the distribution of any hidden part of a 180-day window of tower fluxes, tower meteorology, reanalysis and Sentinel-2 image embeddings, with attention over the days of each variable and over the variables of each day. It is trained on {{n_train}} towers. We also present FluxFill-Bench, {{release_windows}} windows at {{release_sites}} test towers that lie at least 50 km from any training tower, with outages from 2 hours to 180 days hidden identically for every method. At the test towers, the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) of FluxFill is {{test_nee_cmp}} than that of the best established method for net ecosystem exchange (NEE), {{test_le_cmp}} for latent heat (LE), {{test_h_cmp}} for sensible heat (H) and {{test_fch4_cmp}} for methane (FCH4), and its 95% intervals contain {{test_nee_ff_cov95}} of hidden NEE values. For every method with independent samples, the 95% intervals of 30-day sums contain at most {{cov95_sum_30_day_all_max}} of true sums; on validation windows, a temporal copula raises FluxFill's coverage of 30-day sums from 0.363 to 0.960.

01

Introduction

Eddy-covariance towers record the half-hourly fluxes that constrain the carbon, water and energy budgets of ecosystems [[Baldocchi2003, Pastorello2020]]. These records feed annual budgets, model benchmarks and the machine-learning products that map fluxes from towers to continents [[Jung2020]]. Methane records from wetlands, rice paddies and permafrost constrain the largest uncertain term of the global methane budget [[Saunois2020, Delwiche2021]]. Every record has gaps from power and sensor failures, maintenance, and the rejection of values measured under weak turbulence; across the towers of an early synthesis, annual data coverage averaged 65% [[Falge2001]].

Gap filling turns these records into continuous series. The FLUXNET standard, marginal distribution sampling (MDS), replaces a missing value with the mean of values measured under similar radiation, temperature and vapour pressure deficit within a sliding window of days [[Reichstein2005, Wutzler2018]]. Machine-learning methods fit a regression of each flux on meteorological drivers at one tower: artificial neural networks [[Moffat2007]], random forests [[Kim2020, Irvin2021]], gradient-boosted trees [[Liu2025]] and deep ensembles [[Vekuri2025]]. Three properties limit them. They model one flux at a time from drivers measured by the same station, and a station outage removes fluxes and drivers together. They learn from one tower, so a tower with a short record has little to learn from. And their uncertainty, where they report it, has been evaluated on artificial gaps drawn like a tower's real gaps [[Irvin2021]]. In the records used here, {{gap_count_lt1d}}% of station outages are shorter than one day, and {{gap_share_ge7d}}% of the time lost to outages lies in outages of 7 days or longer.

The uncertainty that gap filling adds to annual NEE grows with the length of the gap [[Richardson2007]], and long gaps leave per-tower regressions the least training data. A model trained on many towers can carry what it learns about ecosystem responses across sites. Masked modelling, in which a network learns to reconstruct hidden parts of its input, is the training objective of current foundation models for images [[He2022]] and for time series [[Ansari2025]]. Applied to tower records, it yields a model of the joint distribution of every measured variable, which can fill any pattern of missing values.

Here we present FluxFill, a masked generative model of 180-day windows of tower records, and FluxFill-Bench, a benchmark built from the outages that occur in those records. We score FluxFill and seven established methods on identical windows and masks with the CRPS, a proper scoring rule for probabilistic predictions [[Gneiting2007]], and with the calibration of their intervals for half-hourly values and for sums.

02

Results

A benchmark built from real outages

The dataset holds every tower with measured half-hourly data since 2014 in the FLUXNET Shuttle, FLUXNET-CH4, AmeriFlux and ICOS releases: {{sites_included}} towers of {{sites_candidate}} candidates, {{sites_fch4}} of which measure methane (Fig. 2a). Only values that the source flags as measured enter, as inputs or as targets; values that a source gap-filled never enter. Towers are split by 50 km spatial blocks into {{n_train}} training, {{n_val}} validation and {{n_test}} test towers. At the training towers, {{outage_n}} station outages remove every tower channel for {{outage_years}} tower-years in total, and {{gap_share_ge30d}}% of that time lies in outages of 30 days or longer (Fig. 2b).

FluxFill-Bench release {{release_version}} fixes {{release_windows}} windows of 180 days at {{release_sites}} test towers, one per start season where the record allows, before any model is fitted. Each window carries masks from six families (Methods): gaps drawn to reproduce the tower's own gap-length distribution [[Irvin2021]] (M1); one contiguous block of 2 hours to 180 days hiding every tower channel at once, as a station outage does (M2); the real joint outage of all tower channels transplanted from another site-year (M3); the reanalysis drivers removed together with a 30-day tower outage (M4); and independent blocks per variable together with one whole modality (M5) (Fig. 2c). Every method receives the same window with the hidden values removed, together with the tower's other calendar years, and returns at least 64 samples per hidden value. A generative model of the tower record

FluxFill is a self-supervised AI foundation model trained to reconstruct masked and missing flux data, based on the DeepEarth neural network architecture. It represents a window as one token per variable and day, holding the 48 half-hourly values and 48 flags that mark which of them are visible (Fig. 1b). The DeepEarth network alternates attention over the 180 days of each variable with attention across the variables of each day, so a missing flux is predicted from its own visible days, from the other variables of the same day and from the Clay embeddings of Sentinel-2 images [[Clay2024]] taken near that day. Three channels of each token give the tower's measured mean and spread at the same half-hour and season in its other years. The output head predicts, for every hidden half-hour, a mixture of two Student-t distributions: one anchored at the local diurnal course of the visible days around the gap, one at the tower's other years, with a weight that depends on the distance to the nearest visible value. The network is trained on {{n_train}} towers to predict hidden values under masks drawn from the benchmark families, with whole modalities hidden in some windows.

Calibration of values and of sums

The 95% intervals of FluxFill contain {{test_nee_ff_cov95}} of hidden NEE values, {{test_le_ff_cov95}} of LE, {{test_h_ff_cov95}} of H and {{test_fch4_ff_cov95}} of FCH4 values at the test towers, and its 50% and 80% intervals are equally close to their nominal levels (Fig. 5a). MDS, XGBoost and the deep ensemble under-cover at all three levels, and SAITS over-covers at the 50% and 80% levels. For sums, every method under-covers. The 95% intervals of 30-day sums contain {{cov95_sum_30_day_fluxfill}} of true sums for FluxFill, and between {{cov95_sum_30_day_min}} ({{cov95_sum_30_day_min_name}}) and {{cov95_sum_30_day_max}} ({{cov95_sum_30_day_max_name}}) for the established methods. For sums over whole windows the shares are {{cov95_sum_window_fluxfill}} for FluxFill and between {{cov95_sum_window_min}} ({{cov95_sum_window_min_name}}) and {{cov95_sum_window_max}} ({{cov95_sum_window_max_name}}) for the others (Fig. 5b, c).

Our hypothesis for the cause is the dependence of errors in time. The errors of samples drawn independently for each half-hour average out over a sum. The errors of a fill are correlated over days to weeks: a fill that misses the level of a flux misses it for much of the gap. We measure this dependence on validation windows, from the probability integral transform of each hidden value under FluxFill's predictive distribution, and couple the samples in time with a Gaussian copula fitted to it (Methods). The copula leaves the distribution of each half-hourly value unchanged and changes only the distribution of sums. On validation windows it raises the 95% coverage of 30-day sums from 0.36 to 0.96, so the final model uses it.

Where the model gains

FluxFill's skill over the best established method is positive in every vegetation class with at least three test towers (Fig. 6). For NEE it is largest in open shrublands ({{igbp_skill_open_shrubland_nee}}%), savannas ({{igbp_skill_savanna_nee}}%) and grasslands ({{igbp_skill_grassland_nee}}%), and smallest in evergreen broadleaf forests ({{igbp_skill_evergreen_broadleaf_nee}}%) and deciduous broadleaf forests ({{igbp_skill_deciduous_broadleaf_nee}}%). One explanation is that forest NEE follows the diurnal and seasonal course of light, which climatology and per-tower regressions capture, while NEE in shrublands, savannas and grasslands also responds over days to rain and soil moisture, which the other variables of the window and the Sentinel-2 embeddings record. Table 4 tests part of this explanation: at width 192 and on one seed, removing the Sentinel-2 embeddings leaves overall CRPS unchanged, so the role of imagery in it has no support yet; that test has not been repeated on a second seed, at full size or by vegetation class.

03

Discussion

A single network trained on {{n_train}} towers has a lower CRPS than every established method for NEE, LE and H at every gap length from 1 to 180 days and under real outages. It does not lead everywhere: XGBoost leads the short gaps of family M1, Chronos-2 leads LE at 2 and 6 hours and H at 6 hours, and the per-tower deep ensemble leads the mixed per-variable gaps of family M5, where one explanation is that its tower drivers often stay visible. It predicts each flux from every other measured variable of the same window, so a flux that is missing while radiation and temperature sensors work is predicted from them, and a full station outage falls back on reanalysis, imagery and the tower's other years. Table 4 measures these inputs at a small size: site memory has the largest effect so far, and the Sentinel-2 embeddings have shown none.

Methane is where other methods compete most closely. FluxFill's pooled FCH4 CRPS is {{test_fch4_cmp}} than the best established method at the test towers and {{val_fch4_cmp}} at the validation towers, and it leads FCH4 at 2 of 11 gap types: Chronos-2, the random forest ensemble and XGBoost lead the others. FCH4 is measured at {{fch4_test}} test towers and {{fch4_train}} training towers in the frozen split, and its response to temperature, water table and substrate differs between wetlands more than the response of NEE to light differs between forests. It also holds 0.4% of the measured tower values, so a loss that counts every hidden value equally gives it little weight; averaging the loss per variable lowers FCH4 CRPS by about 16% on two seeds in Table 4, and that change enters the next network. To validate methane filling on more towers, we drew a second split that moves 13 spatial blocks of training towers to validation, matching the ecosystem mix of the methane test towers, and retrained the network on it. On the {{v2_windows}} of its 102 methane validation windows that every method fills, at 18 towers, FluxFill has a FCH4 CRPS {{v2_fch4_a0_cmp}} than the {{v2_fch4_base}}, the best established method there, with 95% intervals that contain {{v2_fch4_cov95}} of hidden values against {{v2_fch4_base_cov95}}. On the same windows its CRPS is {{v2_nee_cmp}} than the best established method for NEE, {{v2_le_cmp}} for LE and {{v2_h_cmp}} for H.

Sums are the open problem for every method. A carbon budget needs the uncertainty of an annual or seasonal sum, and that uncertainty depends on how errors persist in time. The benchmark scores sums from each method's samples, so independent per-step samples are scored on the sums they imply; under that test, with independent samples, no method reaches nominal coverage. A temporal copula of the marginals is the smallest change that addresses this. On the 50 validation windows it raises FluxFill's 95% coverage of 30-day sums from 0.363 to 0.960 and of window sums from 0.361 to 0.965, lowers the CRPS of those sums by 18.6% and 18.9%, and leaves the CRPS of half-hourly values unchanged; the other methods cover these sums at 0.15 to 0.45 by flux. FluxFill then has the lowest CRPS of sums for NEE, LE and H; on FCH4 the RF ensemble and MDS are lower. The copula was fitted on these windows, so the final test scoring measures the gain. The masked generation of joint samples that FluxFill's flow head supports is the more general solution.

Three limits of this study set the next experiments. The tower's other years enter as closed-form statistics, and the model does not reconstruct them at half-hourly resolution; a model of the full multi-year record, in which every year can be hidden and reconstructed, would use them directly. The drivers are the ERA5 fields that ONEFlux downscales and ERA5-Land at 0.1°; sharper regional reanalyses lower the error of air temperature, vapour pressure deficit and wind at most US towers we checked. And the test scores in this version are from the network trained for 24,000 steps; the final network is scored once on the same windows.

Methods

Notation

A window of tower \(w\) is a tensor \(\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times D\times K}\) of \(V\) variables, \(D=180\) days and \(K=48\) half-hours, with an observation indicator \(\mathbf{O}\in\{0,1\}^{V\times D\times K}\) that is 1 where the source measured the value. A mask \(\mathbf{M}\in\{0,1\}^{V\times D\times K}\), drawn from a distribution \(\mathcal{M}\) over the benchmark families, hides values. The hidden set and the visible set are

\[\mathcal{H}=\{(v,d,k): O_{vdk}=1,\ M_{vdk}=1\},\qquad \mathcal{V}=\{(v,d,k): O_{vdk}=1,\ M_{vdk}=0\}.\]

Only \(\mathbf{X}_{\mathcal{V}}\), the imagery embeddings \(\mathbf{C}\), the site-memory statistics \((\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\mathrm{mem}},\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\mathrm{mem}},\mathbf{n}^{\mathrm{mem}})\) of the tower's other years and the site location \(\mathbf{s}\) are given to a method; every score is computed on \(\mathcal{H}\).

Eddy-covariance fluxes

Each half-hourly flux is the covariance, over 30 minutes, of the vertical wind speed \(w\) measured by a sonic anemometer at 10-20 Hz with a scalar measured by a gas analyser or by the anemometer itself [[Baldocchi2003]]. With \(x'=x-\overline{x}\) the departure of a 10-20 Hz sample from its 30-minute mean,

\[F_c=\overline{\rho}_d\,\overline{w's_c'},\qquad \mathrm{NEE}=F_c+\int_0^{z_m}\frac{\partial\overline{c}}{\partial t}\,dz,\qquad \mathrm{LE}=\lambda\,\overline{\rho}_d\,\overline{w's_v'},\qquad H=\overline{\rho}_a\,c_p\,\overline{w'T_s'},\qquad F_{\mathrm{CH_4}}=\overline{\rho}_d\,\overline{w's_m'},\]

where \(s_c\), \(s_v\) and \(s_m\) are the dry mole fractions of CO2, water vapour and CH4, \(T_s\) the sonic temperature, \(\overline{\rho}_d\) and \(\overline{\rho}_a\) the mean molar density of dry air and the density of air, \(\lambda\) the latent heat of vaporisation, \(c_p\) the specific heat of air, \(c\) the CO2 concentration and \(z_m\) the measurement height; the integral is the change of CO2 stored below the sensor. These covariances require stationary, well-mixed turbulence, so values measured under weak turbulence are rejected by quality control, and a power or sensor failure removes all of them at once. FluxFill-Bench and FluxFill work with the half-hourly fluxes that the networks publish.

Tower data

The dataset merges the ONEFlux v1.3 products of the FLUXNET Shuttle [[Pastorello2020]], the FLUXNET-CH4 Community Product V1.0 [[Delwiche2021, Knox2019]], AmeriFlux BASE-BADM for towers that measure methane and ICOS ETC L2 fluxes. For each tower, variable and calendar year one source is used, in a fixed order of preference. A value enters only if the source measured it: from ONEFlux only values with quality flag 0, and from the other sources only the unfilled columns. GPP and ecosystem respiration are partitioning outputs and are excluded. Quality control applies, in order: the steady-state and integral-turbulence flag of the source [[Foken2004]]; the AmeriFlux physical limits of each variable; a test for stuck sensors (runs of identical values of at least 6 hours); the double-difference spike test [[Papale2006]] in 13-day blocks, day and night separately, z = 5.5; friction-velocity filtering of FCH4, and of NEE from BASE and ICOS, at the ONEFlux variable threshold or the moving-point test [[Reichstein2005, Papale2006]]; and record-level tests of the sign of each flux against its driver. After quality control the dataset holds {{nee_measured}} measured half-hours of NEE, {{le_measured}} of LE, {{h_measured}} of H and {{fch4_measured}} of FCH4.

Drivers and imagery

Two reanalysis modalities stay available when the tower's sensors fail: the ERA5 [[Hersbach2020]] fields that ONEFlux downscales to each tower by regression on its measurements [[Vuichard2015]], and ERA5-Land at 0.1° [[MunozSabater2021]] for air temperature, vapour pressure deficit, soil water, soil temperature and snow depth, with ERA5 at 0.25° for shortwave radiation, precipitation, wind and pressure. Pressure is moved hydrostatically to the tower elevation from the Copernicus DEM at 30 m [[European2021]]. The imagery modality holds every Sentinel-2 Level-2A acquisition [[Drusch2012]] since the start of the mission over 300 m × 300 m centred on each tower, every band at its native resolution, with the Sen2Cor scene classification [[MainKnorn2017]]; {{s2_chips}} chips in total. Each chip is embedded by the Clay v1.5 encoder [[Clay2024, Xiong2024]], and the 1,024-dimensional class token is stored with the chip's cloud, shadow and clear fractions.

Split, windows and masks

Towers within 50 km of each other are linked, and each linked group goes wholly to training, validation or test, stratified by IGBP class, Köppen class [[Beck2023]] and methane measurement (seed 20261026; SHA-256 of the split file {{split_sha}}…). A window is 180 days of one tower at half-hourly resolution, 8,640 steps. Release {{release_version}} of FluxFill-Bench holds {{release_windows}} test windows at {{release_sites}} test towers, up to four per tower, one per start season, each the window of that season with the most measured flux. Masks apply only to measured values and are drawn with a seed fixed per tower, window and family; the runner recomputes each mask and refuses to score a window whose mask differs from the release. M1 draws gaps whose lengths, merged with the real gaps, reproduce the tower's gap-length distribution [[Irvin2021]]. M2 hides every tower channel over one block of 2 hours, 6 hours, 1, 7, 30, 90 or 180 days with a uniform start. M3 applies the joint outage of all tower channels from a training site-year. M4 removes the reanalysis modality for the window together with a 30-day M2 block. M5 hides an independent block of log-uniform length between one half-hour and 90 days in each variable, and one whole modality. Every method receives the window with the hidden values removed and the tower's other calendar years; the window's own years never enter the context.

Scores

The primary score is the CRPS [[Gneiting2007]], computed from samples with the fair estimator [[Ferro2014]] or in closed form where a method gives a parametric distribution [[Jordan2019]]. Calibration is the coverage of central 50%, 80% and 95% intervals [[Gneiting2007a]]. Sums of each flux over the hidden half-hours of each day, each 30-day block and the whole window are formed per sample and scored with CRPS and coverage. Point scores of the predictive mean are the MAE, RMSE, bias, R² and the weighted absolute percentage error, 100 × Σ|prediction − truth| / Σ|truth|. A cell is one tower, window, mask family and variable; every comparison between methods pools only the cells that all compared methods fill completely, weighting each cell by its number of hidden values, and a method enters a comparison when it fills at least 90% of the hidden values of that family.

\[\mathrm{CRPS}(F,y)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(F(z)-\mathbb{1}\{y\le z\}\big)^2\,dz,\qquad \widehat{\mathrm{CRPS}}=\frac{1}{S}\sum_{i=1}^{S}|x^{(i)}-y|-\frac{1}{2S(S-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}|x^{(i)}-x^{(j)}|,\]
\[\Sigma^{(i)}_B=\sum_{(d,k)\in B}x^{(i)}_{vdk}\ \ \text{for each sample } i \text{ and block } B,\qquad R^2=1-\frac{\sum_{\mathcal{H}}(\hat{x}-x)^2}{\sum_{\mathcal{H}}(x-\bar{x})^2},\qquad \mathrm{WAPE}=100\,\frac{\sum_{\mathcal{H}}|\hat{x}-x|}{\sum_{\mathcal{H}}|x|},\]

with \(S=64\) samples \(x^{(i)}\) and the predictive mean \(\hat{x}\). The coverage of a central interval of level \(q\) is the share of hidden values with \((1-q)/2\le F(y)\le(1+q)/2\), computed from the exact predictive CDF \(F\) where a method gives one.

Established methods

Every method implements one interface and runs on the same windows and masks; code, versions and settings are listed on the Methods tab. Climatology is the empirical distribution of the variable at the same tower, half-hour and season (±15 days) in its other years. MDS [[Reichstein2005]] runs through REddyProc 1.3.2 [[Wutzler2018]] on the visible part of the window. The random forest ensemble is the released harness of Irvin et al. [[Irvin2021]], with its Platt factor fitted on held-out blocks of the tower's other years. The deep ensemble is the five-member network of Vekuri et al. [[Vekuri2025]], ported to PyTorch. XGBoost follows Liu et al. [[Liu2025]] with Clay principal components in place of PhenoCam greenness and multi-quantile regression for uncertainty. SAITS and CSDI [[Du2023, Tashiro2021]] are trained on {{pypots_sites}} training towers with block masks. Chronos-2 [[Ansari2025]] forecasts each hidden block from the half-hours before it, with the ERA5 drivers as covariates.

Network

A day-token holds one variable's 48 standardised values and 48 visible flags, with three site-memory channels: the mean, the standard deviation and the count of the tower's measured values at the same half-hour within ±15 days of the same day of year in its other years. A shared linear map embeds it, and learned embeddings of the variable, the provenance (tower, reanalysis, reanalysis-land, imagery), the day of the window and the tower location, and harmonics of the day of year, are added; a token without visible values is replaced by a learned mask vector. The imagery token of a day is the Clay class token of that day's chip with its scene-classification fractions. The encoder has {{depth}} blocks of width {{d}} with {{heads}} heads [[Vaswani2017]], alternating time attention, with a rotary encoding of the day index [[Su2021]], and variable attention [[Ho2019]]; it has {{model_params}} parameters. The output head predicts, for each half-hour, a mixture of two Student-t distributions [[Kendall2017]]. The first component is anchored at the local mean diurnal course of the visible days within the window and continues the anomaly of the nearest visible value with a learned decay per variable. The second is anchored at the site-memory mean plus the window's departure from the tower's other years. The mixture weight and the scales depend on the encoder state and on the local evidence: the count of visible local values and the distance to the nearest one. A flow-matching head [[Lipman2023, Li2024]] trained jointly provides joint samples by masked generation [[Chang2022]]; the scores reported here use the Student-t mixture.

\[\mathbf{h}=f_\theta\big(\mathbf{X}\odot\mathbf{O}\odot(1-\mathbf{M}),\ \mathbf{O}\odot(1-\mathbf{M}),\ \boldsymbol{\mu}^{\mathrm{mem}},\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\mathrm{mem}},\mathbf{n}^{\mathrm{mem}},\ \mathbf{C},\ \mathbf{s}\big),\qquad \mathbf{h}_{vd}\in\mathbb{R}^{320}\]
\[p_\theta(x_{vdk}\mid\mathbf{h}_{vd})=\pi_{vdk}\;t_{\nu^{1}_{vdk}}\!\big(x_{vdk};\,b^{1}_{vdk}+m^{1}_{vdk},\,s^{1}_{vdk}\big)+(1-\pi_{vdk})\;t_{\nu^{2}_{vdk}}\!\big(x_{vdk};\,b^{2}_{vdk}+m^{2}_{vdk},\,s^{2}_{vdk}\big)\]

Here \(t_\nu(\cdot;\,\mu,s)\) is the Student-t density with \(\nu>2\) degrees of freedom, location \(\mu\) and scale \(s\); \(m\), \(s\), \(\nu\) and the weight \(\pi\) are linear functions of \(\mathbf{h}_{vd}\) and of the local evidence. The anchors are the local course and the memory course,

\[b^{1}_{vdk}=\bar{x}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{vk}+\rho_v^{\,\Delta_{vdk}}\,a^{\mathrm{edge}}_{v},\qquad b^{2}_{vdk}=\mu^{\mathrm{mem}}_{vdk}+c^{\mathrm{near}}_{v}A^{\mathrm{near}}_{vdk}+c^{\mathrm{win}}_{v}A^{\mathrm{win}}_{vk},\]

where \(\bar{x}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{vk}\) is the mean of visible values at half-hour \(k\) on nearby days, \(a^{\mathrm{edge}}_v\) the anomaly of the nearest visible value, \(\Delta_{vdk}\) the distance to it in half-hours, \(\rho_v\in(0,1)\) a learned decay, and \(A\) the departures of the window's visible values from the memory mean, near the gap and over the window.

Training

Training windows are sampled with weights inverse to the size of each IGBP and Köppen stratum. Each training window receives a mask drawn from the benchmark families and from random blocks per variable; some windows hide every tower channel, and whole modalities are hidden in others. The loss is the negative log-likelihood of hidden measured values under the Student-t mixture, plus the flow-matching loss and the Gaussian likelihood of hidden imagery embeddings. The final network was trained for 48,000 steps of eight windows with AdamW, a peak learning rate of 3 × 10−4 after 500 warm-up steps and cosine decay, at {{train_s_per_step}} seconds per step on one RTX 3090. Every choice of architecture and training was made on validation CRPS; test towers were not used until a model was final.

With \(\mathbf{r}_{vd}=\mathbf{x}_{vd}-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\mathrm{mem}}_{vd}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}\) the residual of a day-token from site memory, \(\mathbf{m}_{vd}\in\{0,1\}^{K}\) its hidden-and-measured indicator, \(\mathcal{H}_I\) the hidden imagery days, \(\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_d\) the layer-normalised Clay embedding and \(\omega_v\) a weight per variable (1 in training), the objective is

\[\begin{aligned}\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\ \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{O})\sim\mathcal{D},\ \mathbf{M}\sim\mathcal{M}}\Bigg[&\underbrace{\frac{\sum_{(v,d,k)\in\mathcal{H}}\omega_v\big(-\log p_\theta(x_{vdk}\mid\mathbf{h}_{vd})\big)}{\sum_{(v,d,k)\in\mathcal{H}}\omega_v}}_{\text{half-hourly values}}\;+\;\lambda_{\mathrm{img}}\underbrace{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}_I|}\sum_{d\in\mathcal{H}_I}-\log\mathcal{N}\big(\tilde{\mathbf{c}}_d;\,\boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta(\mathbf{h}_{Id}),\,\mathrm{diag}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2_\theta(\mathbf{h}_{Id})\big)}_{\text{imagery}}\\&+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\mathcal{U}[0,1],\ \boldsymbol{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_K)}\frac{\sum_{(v,d)}\big\|\mathbf{m}_{vd}\odot\big(u_\theta(\mathbf{z}_\tau,\tau,\mathbf{h}_{vd})-(\mathbf{r}_{vd}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon})\big)\big\|_2^2}{\sum_{(v,d)}\|\mathbf{m}_{vd}\|_1}}_{\text{flow matching}}\Bigg],\qquad \mathbf{z}_\tau=(1-\tau)\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}+\tau\,\mathbf{r}_{vd},\end{aligned}\]

with \(\lambda_{\mathrm{img}}=0.1\); the flow term samples at most 2,048 hidden tokens per batch and draws four \((\tau,\boldsymbol{\epsilon})\) pairs for each. The parameters are updated by AdamW [[Kingma2015, Loshchilov2019]] with decoupled weight decay and the gradient norm clipped at 1,

\[\hat{\mathbf{g}}_t=\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}_t}(\theta_{t-1})\cdot\min\!\Big(1,\frac{1}{\|\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}_t}(\theta_{t-1})\|_2}\Big),\qquad \theta_t=\theta_{t-1}-\eta_t\Big(\frac{\hat{\mathbf{m}}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf{v}}_t}+\varepsilon}+\lambda_{\mathrm{wd}}\,\theta_{t-1}\Big),\]

where \(\hat{\mathbf{m}}_t\) and \(\hat{\mathbf{v}}_t\) are the bias-corrected first and second moments of \(\hat{\mathbf{g}}_t\) with \(\beta_1=0.9\), \(\beta_2=0.98\), \(\lambda_{\mathrm{wd}}=0.05\), \(\varepsilon=10^{-8}\), and \(\eta_t\) rises linearly to \(3\times10^{-4}\) over 500 steps and then follows a cosine decay.

Temporal copula

For each hidden value the probability integral transform u = F(y) under FluxFill's predictive distribution is computed exactly, and mapped to a normal score Φ−1(u). On the validation windows, the autocorrelation of normal scores at lags from one half-hour to 30 days is pooled over all hidden blocks of each flux and fitted by least squares with ρ(k) = w e−k/Ls + (1 − w) e−k/Lf. Samples are then drawn by generating, per variable, the sum of two stationary first-order autoregressive Gaussian processes with these e-folding lengths and weights, mapping them to uniforms and through the inverse predictive distribution of each half-hour; the mixture component is chosen by a second field with the same law. Each half-hour's marginal distribution is unchanged, which we verified by Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests on synthetic predictive distributions. This is the Gaussian-copula form of ensemble copula coupling [[Schefzik2013, Clark2004]].

\[g_{vt}=\sqrt{w_v}\,a^{\mathrm{s}}_{vt}+\sqrt{1-w_v}\,a^{\mathrm{f}}_{vt},\qquad a_{vt}=\rho\,a_{v,t-1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,\epsilon_{vt},\ \ \rho=e^{-1/L},\ \ \epsilon_{vt}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),\qquad x^{(i)}_{vt}=F_{vt}^{-1}\big(\Phi(g^{(i)}_{vt})\big),\]

so that \(\mathrm{corr}(g_{vt},g_{v,t+k})=w_v e^{-k/L^{\mathrm{s}}_v}+(1-w_v)e^{-k/L^{\mathrm{f}}_v}\) and each \(x^{(i)}_{vt}\) has the marginal distribution \(F_{vt}\).

Runtime

The runtime benchmark runs on one workstation: AMD Ryzen 5 2600 (six cores, 12 threads), 128 GB of memory, two NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs, Ubuntu with Linux 6.8. Each method runs alone, pinned to six CPU cores, with deep-learning methods on one GPU and the statistical and tree methods on six CPU threads. It has not yet run. The runner records the wall-clock seconds of each mask's prediction, including any per-window fitting.
