FluxFill · report draft

A masked generative model of eddy-covariance tower records, and a benchmark for filling their gaps

Mira Shivani Sankar, Lance Legel, Trevor Keenan. Quantitative Ecosystem Dynamics lab, University of California, Berkeley. Sponsored by Ecological Intelligence, Inc.

FluxFill learns the joint distribution of everything measured at and around {{sites_included}} eddy-covariance towers, over 180-day windows at half-hourly resolution, and fills any missing part of a record with calibrated samples. FluxFill-Bench scores it and 8 baseline methods on identical windows and masks with proper scoring rules, at gaps from one day to the whole window.

01

Data

Towers

Every tower with measured half-hourly data since 2014. The dataset merges four sources: the ONEFlux v1.3 products of the FLUXNET Shuttle (AmeriFlux, ICOS with the European Fluxes Database, ChinaFlux, JapanFlux, KoFlux and SAEON, and TERN) [[Pastorello2020]]; the FLUXNET-CH4 Community Product V1.0 [[Delwiche2021, Knox2019]]; AmeriFlux BASE-BADM for sites that measure methane; and ICOS ETC L2 fluxes. Of {{sites_candidate}} candidate towers, {{sites_included}} have at least one usable 180-day window and enter the dataset; {{sites_fch4}} of them measure methane. All tower data are licensed CC-BY-4.0. For each site, variable and calendar year one source is used, in a fixed order of preference, and the agreement of overlapping sources is recorded.

Only measured values. A value enters only if the source measured it. Gap-filled values (MDS, ANN or ERA-downscaled) never enter, as targets or as inputs: from ONEFlux only values with quality flag 0 are read, and from FLUXNET-CH4, BASE and ICOS only the unfilled columns. GPP and ecosystem respiration are partitioning outputs and are excluded. After quality control the dataset holds {{nee_measured}} measured half-hours of NEE, {{le_measured}} of LE, {{h_measured}} of H and {{fch4_measured}} of FCH4.

Quality control. After the measured-only rule, six rules run in order, each a function in the dataset code, and each removed value is counted under its rule: the source's steady-state and integral-turbulence flag [[Foken2004]]; the AmeriFlux physical limits of each variable; stuck sensors (runs of identical values of at least 6 hours, longer for slow variables); the double-difference spike test of Papale et al. [[Papale2006]] in 13-day blocks, day and night separately, z = 5.5; friction-velocity filtering of FCH4 and of NEE from BASE and ICOS at the ONEFlux variable threshold or the moving point test [[Reichstein2005, Papale2006]]; and record-level tests of the sign of each flux against its driver. Times are local standard time, as published; a clock test on each source-year compares the diurnal cycle of incoming shortwave radiation with solar elevation.

Split. Sites are split 80/20 into training and test, by site, with seed 20261026. Sites within 50 km of each other are linked, and each linked group goes wholly to one side, so no test site lies within 50 km of a training or validation site. The split is stratified by IGBP group, Köppen main class (network metadata, else the 1-km maps of Beck et al. [[Beck2023]]) and methane measurement. A validation set of 10% of the training sites, drawn the same way, serves all model selection. The result: {{n_train}} training, {{n_val}} validation and {{n_test}} test sites, of which {{fch4_train}}, {{fch4_val}} and {{fch4_test}} measure methane. The split file is frozen and hashed (SHA-256 {{split_sha}}…).

Windows. A window is 180 days of one site at half-hourly resolution, 8,640 steps. At a 30-day stride the dataset holds {{windows_all}} windows, of which {{windows_usable}} are usable (at least 20% of steps with a measured NEE, LE, H or FCH4). Sampling weights are inverse to the size of each IGBP group × Köppen stratum, capped at 5, so that well-studied strata do not dominate.

Drivers

Reanalysis at every tower. Two reanalysis modalities stay available when the tower's own sensors fail. The first is the ERA5 [[Hersbach2020]] downscaled to each site by ONEFlux, with a regression on the tower's own measurements [[Vuichard2015]]; it is carried in the tower files. The second is ERA5-Land at 0.1° [[MunozSabater2021]] for air temperature, vapour pressure deficit, soil water and soil temperature in two layers and snow depth, joined with ERA5 at 0.25° for shortwave radiation, precipitation, wind and pressure (the fields that force ERA5-Land). Pressure is moved hydrostatically to the tower elevation from the Copernicus DEM at 30 m [[European2021]], which lowers its hourly RMSE against tower pressure from 1.77 to 0.59 kPa at 33 towers (2019-2020).

Sharper drivers, measured against the towers. HRRR at 3 km [[Dowell2022]] has lower hourly RMSE than ERA5-Land for air temperature at 10 of 12 CONUS towers, for vapour pressure deficit at 10 of 12 and for wind at 11 of 12, and higher RMSE for shortwave radiation at all 12. No gridded precipitation source (IMERG Late [[Huffman2023]], MRMS, HRRR) has a higher daily correlation with the tower gauges than ERA5. HRRR is validated but is not yet a model input.

Sentinel-2

Every acquisition, every band, at native resolution. The imagery modality holds every Sentinel-2 Level-2A acquisition [[Drusch2012]] since the start of the mission over a 300 m × 300 m area centred on each tower: bands B02, B03, B04 and B08 at 10 m (30 × 30 pixels), B05, B06, B07, B8A, B11 and B12 at 20 m, B01 and B09 at 60 m, and the Sen2Cor scene classification [[MainKnorn2017]]. No acquisition is removed for cloud; the scene classification gives each chip its cloud, shadow, snow and clear fractions. The source is the Collection 1 reprocessing on Earth Search, with the Microsoft Planetary Computer for acquisitions Collection 1 does not hold, one product per acquisition. Digital numbers are stored unchanged with the radiometric offset of their processing baseline. The archive holds {{s2_chips}} chips; {{s2_sites}} towers are complete.

Clay embeddings

One embedding per chip. Every chip is embedded by the Clay v1.5 encoder [[Clay2024]], a ViT-L with 24 layers and patch size 8 whose patch embedding is conditioned on wavelength [[Xiong2024]]; v1.5 is the latest release. The ten Clay bands are harmonised across processing baselines, the 20 m bands are repeated to 10 m, and the 30 × 30 pixel chip is edge-padded to 32 × 32 pixels at its true 10 m sampling. The padded embedding agrees with the embedding of a true 32 × 32 pixel read at a mean cosine of 0.978 at US-Ha1 and 0.981 at DE-Tha. The 1,024-dimensional CLS token is stored for every chip with its scene-classification fractions; {{clay_sites}} towers have embeddings.

02

Benchmark

Frozen before fitting. FluxFill-Bench fixes test sites, held-out years and masks before any model is fitted, and records their SHA-256 hashes. Release {{release_version}} holds {{release_windows}} test windows at {{release_sites}} of the {{n_test}} test sites, with mask families {{release_families}}; the runner recomputes every mask and refuses to score a window whose mask differs from the release. Up to four windows per test site are chosen, one per start season, each the window with the most measured flux.

Three regimes. R1 is a gap inside a record at a test site, with the window's unmasked observations visible. R2 is the held-out final year at a training site; that year is excluded from training. R3 is a test window with every tower channel masked for the whole window, so only reanalysis and imagery remain. No method may be tuned, calibrated or checkpoint-selected on test sites or held-out years.

Four mask families. M1 reproduces each site's observed gap-length distribution [[Irvin2021]], for continuity with published numbers. M2 hides all tower channels over one contiguous block of 1, 7, 30, 90 or 180 days with a uniform start, as a station outage does. M3 transplants the real joint outage of all tower channels from another site-year; its donor library comes from training sites only. M4 removes one modality for the whole window: tower meteorology, reanalysis or Sentinel-2. Masks apply only where the truth is measured, and each is drawn with a seed fixed per site, window and family.

Proper scores first. The primary score is the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) [[Gneiting2007]], computed from 64 samples per window with the fair ensemble estimator [[Ferro2014]] or in closed form for parametric predictions [[Jordan2019]]. Calibration is the coverage of the central 50%, 80% and 95% intervals [[Gneiting2007a]], by gap length. Sums of each flux over the masked half-hours of every day, every 30-day block and the whole window are formed per sample and scored with CRPS, bias and coverage, so methods that sample each half-hour independently are scored on the sums they imply. Point scores of the predictive mean (MAE, MSE, RMSE, bias, R² and the weighted absolute percentage error) are reported for comparison with the literature. Every number names its regime, mask family, gap length, variable and the number of values it rests on. Gap lengths are also classed in half-hours (1; 2 to 8; 9 to 64; 65 to 576; more than 576).

03

Baselines

Eight methods, one protocol. Every baseline implements the same interface: it receives a window whose hidden values are removed and the site's other calendar years, and returns at least 64 samples per hidden value. All receive the same windows and masks and are scored by the same code. Model selection uses validation sites only. Each method's code, pinned version and exact settings are on the Methods tab.

Climatology. The empirical distribution of the variable at the same site, the same half-hour of the day and within ±15 days of the same day of year, in every other year of the record. It uses no drivers and is the floor reference.

Marginal distribution sampling. The operational standard of FLUXNET processing [[Reichstein2005]], run through REddyProc 1.3.2 [[Wutzler2018]] on the visible part of the window, with incoming shortwave radiation, air temperature and vapour pressure deficit as drivers (ERA5 where the tower driver is hidden). Its spread is the standard deviation of the values it averages.

Random forest ensemble. The released harness of Irvin et al. [[Irvin2021]] at commit 473db3e: ten random forests on time features and four drivers, with a Laplace predictive distribution scaled by a Platt factor. Two changes are made: the time feature `delta` has a fixed origin at fit and at prediction, and the Platt factor is fitted on held-out blocks of the site's other years, never on the window. One fixed configuration from the released search space replaces the search.

Deep ensemble. The five-member ensemble of Vekuri et al. [[Vekuri2025]] at commit 7edd07a, ported from Keras to PyTorch with the same network, Laplace likelihood, optimiser rule and early stopping. Each sample follows one member's mean trajectory, so sums carry the spread between members.

XGBoost with vegetation state. The XGBoost method of Liu et al. [[Liu2025]] at commit a9a6eeb, with the first eight principal components of the site's Clay embeddings in place of PhenoCam greenness where a site has them, and multi-quantile regression for uncertainty, which the release does not provide.

SAITS and CSDI. Two generic imputers from PyPOTS 1.5 [[Du2023, Tashiro2021]], trained on {{pypots_sites}} training sites with benchmark-like block masks. SAITS ({{saits_params}} parameters) reads the window as 180 day-tokens and gets a residual Gaussian spread from calibration sites. CSDI ({{csdi_params}} parameters) samples half-hourly 6-day chunks by conditional diffusion.

Chronos-2. The pretrained forecasting model of Ansari et al. [[Ansari2025]] ({{chronos_params}} parameters), used zero-shot: each hidden block is forecast from the 2,048 half-hours before it with the ERA5 drivers as known covariates, or from the data after it when there is no history.

04

The FluxFill model

One model of the whole field. FluxFill models a window as a field over 180 days, 48 half-hours per day and {{n_rows}} variable rows (tower fluxes, tower meteorology and soil, ERA5 and ERA5-Land drivers), plus one row of Sentinel-2 imagery. Each value carries an observation flag (measured, hidden, or absent at the source) and a provenance (tower, reanalysis, reanalysis-land, imagery). The model learns the distribution of the hidden values given the visible ones for any pattern of missingness. Every modality can be hidden, so the same model fills a flux, a meteorological sensor or an image.

Tokens. A day-token holds one variable's 48 standardised values and their 48 flags, together with three site-memory channels: the mean, the spread and the presence of the site's measured values at the same half-hour within ±15 days in its other years, excluding the window's own years and the held-out year. A shared linear map embeds the token; learned embeddings of the variable, the provenance, the day of the window and the site location, and harmonics of the day of year, are added. A token with no visible value is a learned mask vector. The imagery token of a day is the mean Clay CLS embedding of that day's chips with their scene-classification fractions.

Encoder. {{depth}} pairs of transformer blocks [[Vaswani2017]] alternate two factorised attentions [[Ho2019]]: time attention, in which each variable attends over its 180 days with a rotary encoding of the day index [[Su2021]], and variable attention, in which each day attends across its variables and its imagery token. Width {{d}}, {{heads}} heads; variables absent from the window and dropped modalities are excluded as keys. The scored checkpoint has {{model_params}} parameters.

Two heads. v0 predicts a Student-t distribution for every half-hour (location, scale and degrees of freedom), trained on the negative log-likelihood [[Kendall2017]]; its location is the site-memory mean plus the head's output. Its samples are independent per half-hour. v1 produces joint samples by masked generation with continuous tokens [[Li2024]]: a flow-matching head [[Lipman2023]], an MLP with adaptive layer norm [[Peebles2023]], generates the 48 values of a hidden day-token conditioned on its encoder state. Hidden tokens are generated in a random order, in {{rounds}} rounds that follow the cosine schedule of MaskGIT [[Chang2022]], and each generated token is fed back as visible before the next round. Independent orders and noise give {{samples}} joint samples per window, whose sums over a day, a month or the window keep the dependence between half-hours. Masked imagery tokens are predicted with a diagonal Gaussian likelihood.

{{arch}}

Training. Training masks are drawn from the benchmark families and from random blocks per variable; some windows hide every tower channel (regime R3), and whole modalities are dropped. The loss is computed only on hidden measured values. Windows are sampled by the stratum weights of the dataset. The scored checkpoint ({{scored_run}}, step {{scored_step}}) was trained for {{train_steps}} steps of {{batch}} windows on the training sites, with AdamW, a learning rate of {{lr}}, {{warmup}} warm-up steps and cosine decay. Every choice was selected on validation-site CRPS; each experiment, its diagnosed cause and its result are listed on the Methods tab.

Ablations for publication. Each is a retrained model scored on the full benchmark: without Sentinel-2, without reanalysis, without tower meteorology, without site memory, with the marginal head only, with per-variable masks only, Clay CLS against patch tokens, each driver set, and window lengths of 90, 180 and 365 days.
