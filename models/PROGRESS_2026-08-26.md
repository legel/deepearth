## 5. Validation — what is actually established

**One real dynamic validation exists**, and its headline was overstated.

Site3 / Gee Creek (USGS 02234400), Hurricane Ian:

| metric | difference | reading |
|---|---|---|
| **rising limb** (50 % of peak) | **0.09 h** | real agreement — flood onset right to ~5 minutes |
| peak argmax | 1.72 h | *inside the resolution limit* — plateaus are 1.60 h (sim) / 3.00 h (obs) wide |
| centroid | **7.44 h** | response centred 7 h early — recedes far too fast |

Measured on the current solver with the storage cap active. The cap improved both meaningful
metrics (rising limb 0.47 → 0.09 h, centroid 9.13 → 7.44 h) and moved the argmax 1.24 → 1.72 h,
which is uninformative either way since it sits inside the plateau width.

The previously-quoted "1.24 h peak-timing error" is smaller than either peak's own plateau and
cannot be claimed as accuracy. The rising-limb result is robust to threshold and baseflow choice.

Johns Lake has **no** viable event validation: the gauge samples ~27-daily (2 usable brackets
in 67 years), and its one satellite-flagged candidate (2024-02-12, +17 ha) had **no rain at five
independent stations** (1.1–3.9 mm). Its `historical_20240212` scenario is not a validation case.

## 6. Physics: one bug diagnosed, fixed, measured

**Infiltration was unbounded.** `horton_rate()` returns a rate decaying to `fc` and staying
there forever; `cum_infil` was tracked but never limited anything — 1,678 mm of capacity against
392 mm of rain.

| | observed | simulated (before) | simulated (after) |
|---|---|---|---|
| runoff coefficient | **19.6 %** | 1.0 % | **1.7 %** |
| peak outflow | 1,190 cfs | 91.4 cfs | 129.8 cfs |
| peak flooded area | — | 127.6 ha | **246.1 ha** |

Fixed by capping cumulative infiltration at SSURGO-derived soil storage (new
`soil/fetch_soil_storage.py`; water-table depth × drainable porosity; mean 206 mm, 26 % of cells
depressional). This is HEC-RAS/HEC-HMS's "maximum deficit" — every standard method
(Deficit-and-Constant, Curve Number, Green-Ampt) is storage-limited; unbounded Horton is the
outlier.

**The fix works and is not the dominant error.** Flooded area nearly doubled, but runoff only
reached 1.7 % — an 11.5× gap remains.

## 7. The dominant remaining error: conveyance, not storage

Measured, after one wrong hypothesis (depression storage) was tested and rejected:

- The conditioned DEM has **240 true pits** (0.00 %). Breaching worked. An earlier count of
  ~750,000 "pits" was a detector artifact conflating *flats* (11.02 %) with pits.
- `fill_pits` and `fill_depressions` both add **zero** volume — nothing to fill.
- 24 h after rain stops, flooded area is still **rising** (+0.13 ha/hr). Extrapolated, the
  standing 244 ha needs **~80 days** to clear.

Water is generated correctly but cannot be conveyed across near-zero gradient to an outlet.
This is the D8 under-capture (11.65 of 33.15 km²) seen from the water side. **Restoring channel
connectivity — not adding storage or tuning infiltration — is the open problem.**
