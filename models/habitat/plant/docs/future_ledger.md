# Future-work ledger

Major upgrades and fundamental opportunities beyond the Daru (2024) model, noted as they arise. Each entry:
what, why it matters, evidence so far, cost.

| # | Opportunity | Why | Evidence / status |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Store the model, not the map: a range = MaxEnt coefficients (a few kB) + one shared predictor stack, rendered on demand on GPU | 10³–10⁴× smaller than per-species rasters; any resolution/tile on request; exact by construction | **Implemented and verified 2026-10-04** (`codec.py`, card format v2): Larrea 5.31 kB vs 13.1 MB of ZSTD GeoTIFFs (2,466×), Echinacea 4.78 kB vs 20.2 MB (4,226×); all three maps decode byte-identical. Shared one-time cost: 7.2 GB CONUS predictor layers + 1.5 MB ecoregion layer. Tile-level decode done (10 ms per 256² tile, byte-identical). |
| L2 | Phylogenetic borrowing of niches (BePhyNE-style multivariate Brownian motion of niche optimum/breadth, or a phylogenetic embedding conditioning a joint model) | 40% of non-native priority species have < 20 US cultivated records; Daru's per-species MaxEnt shares nothing between relatives | literature (2026): no open-source Python implementation exists |
| L3 | Terrain-aware climate downscaling (lapse-rate corrected, 3DEP elevation) to 240 m | WorldClim ~1 km is the climate limit; measured 2026-10-04: aggregating our 240 m Larrea map to 18 km does not lower AIM AUC (0.967 → 0.970), i.e. the current 240 m maps hold no finer information | v1 built (GLO-90 terrain + lapse-rate temperatures, ~230 m/240 m); Larrea: no gain (AUC 0.987 vs 0.986). Panel of 6: mixed (Pinus edulis 0.936 vs 0.925; Festuca 0.847 vs 0.860; Lupinus 0.710 vs 0.720; Eriogonum 0.793 vs 0.801; Purshia 0.794 vs 0.789). Kept as an option, not default. Next: genuinely microclimatic predictors (cold-air pooling, insolation, soil moisture) rather than lapse-rate shifts |
| L4 | Nursery inventories as presence/absence-like evidence (a full inventory that omits a species is informative) with target-group background | Stronger than presence-only for adapted ranges | not part of this model (the inventories are non-public data) |
| L5 | PyTorch MaxEnt fitted for thousands of species at once, validated against maxent.jar | Days → hours; bridge to DeepEarth | **Built and validated 2026-10-04** (`maxent_torch.py`; spec `docs/maxent_fitting_spec.md`; report `docs/maxent_torch.md`). It reimplements maxent.jar 3.4.4's fitting step for step on GPU, with every run of a species in one float64 batch, and writes maxent.jar's files.<br>Against maxent.jar on 34 species (5–5,000 presences, both modes):<br>• same β 34/34;<br>• 850 CV folds: identical feature sets in 99.3%, median max \|Δλ\| 1.3e-12, test AUC identical in 99.6%, worst fold Spearman 0.99996;<br>• final replicates refitted on maxent.jar's own splits: \|Δ ESS\| ≤ 0.0007;<br>• 240 m maps on 9 benchmark species: identical (ρ ≥ 0.99994, binaries ≥ 0.9997 agreement, AIM and VegBank \|Δ AUC\| ≤ 0.00004).<br>With its own replicate draws, maps differ from maxent.jar only as much as two maxent.jar runs differ from each other (maxent.jar seeds its replicates from the clock).<br>Speed (one RTX 3090, shared machine): 21–31 s vs 162–213 s per species, ~9× less CPU.<br>Selectable with `run_fit.py --engine torch`; made the default where a CUDA GPU exists (`--engine auto`) on 2026-10-04 (provenance).<br>Next: a fitting server that batches species across workers (per-iteration launch overhead, not arithmetic, now sets the cost); quadratic and product features if ever needed |
| L6 | Spatial-block CV for β selection (Daru selects on random folds, which favours low regularization under spatial autocorrelation) | Less optimistic estimate of transfer to unsampled areas | — |
| L7 | Hardiness-zone constraint (species' minimum hardiness zone × USDA 2023 Plant Hardiness Zone Map) for adapted maps | Independent physiological cold limit | — |
| L8 | Train on thinned real occurrences (with the effort-weighted background) instead of 500 points spread uniformly over the alpha hull | Daru's hull sampling removes all within-range structure on purpose (a defence against sampling bias at ~18 km); at 240 m it makes the map inside the hull a pure climate envelope. Bias is already handled by the KDE background | **Validated on Larrea tridentata (2026-10-04, BLM AIM, 71,422 plots, 5,234 presences):** AUC 0.986 vs 0.879 (Daru-faithful, ~1 km) and 0.939 (Daru's published ~18 km map); still 0.983 vs 0.964 (Daru) when presences are restricted to plots > 10 km from any training record (n = 245). Full pipeline (5 replicates, thresholds): AUC 0.986 / TSS 0.840 vs Daru 0.939 / 0.723. **Panel (6 western species, BLM AIM): occurrence-trained beats hull-trained for every species** (e.g. Festuca idahoensis 0.860 vs 0.455, Lupinus argenteus 0.720 vs 0.436, Pinus edulis 0.925 vs 0.851); hull training falls to or below chance for widespread species with patchy occupancy. Render-loop benchmark: occurrence-trained vs Daru's published maps, AIM 0.847 vs 0.742 (8 species), FIA 0.906 vs 0.866 (6 of 6 better). **Adopted as the recommended product; `daru` mode kept as the faithful reference** |
| L9 | Restrict nothing to the calibration area for adapted ranges; keep it for native ranges | Daru's calibration mask is what makes his maps *realized* ranges; the adapted (planted) range is a fundamental-niche question | design decided; implement with the adapted product; **pilot 2026-10-04** (`scripts/pilot_adapted.py`, 6 East Asian / European priority-list species fitted on their native ranges, projected over CONUS with no mask, scored on their US GBIF records against US records of other non-native priority-list species): AUC 0.56–0.81 (Lagerstroemia indica 0.809, Hydrangea macrophylla 0.666, Buxus sempervirens 0.642, Acer palmatum 0.596, Ilex crenata 0.594, Nandina domestica 0.564); share of US records inside the P5 range 2% (Acer palmatum, 13% of CONUS) to 88% (Hydrangea, 32%). The native realized niche under-predicts where a species survives when planted: a native-niche projection is one input to the adapted product, not the product. Supports L4 (nursery inventories) and cultivated records as primary evidence |
| L10 | **Priority.** Fine-scale predictors beyond climate (soil from gNATSGO/SoilGrids, terrain from 3DEP: slope, aspect, topographic wetness, heat load; land cover for native ranges) | 240 m output with 1 km climate leaves habitat structure unmodelled (see L3 measurement) | needs global-consistent layers for training outside CONUS (SoilGrids 250 m, Copernicus DEM 90 m); **soil measured 2026-10-04** (SoilGrids 2.0 pH, clay, sand, SOC at 5–15 cm; `soil.py`, predictor set `soil`; 8-species western panel, BLM AIM, occurrence-trained): AUC 0.821 vs 0.793 climate-only (better for 7 of 8), TSS (P5) 0.331 vs 0.277, but 240 m vs 0.96 km still −0.0009: soil improves placement at ≥ 1 km, not below. Caveats: SoilGrids has no data on water, bare rock and built land, so 10% of background points (1.8% of presences) drop and maps hold gaps there; SoilGrids is partly predicted from WorldClim. 40-species east/west test on VegBank (37 paired species, 2026-10-04): AUC 0.9301 vs 0.9298 (soil better for 59%; east −0.001, 47%; west +0.002, 72%), TSS (P5) 0.766 vs 0.756 (70%), sub-kilometre gain −0.0005 vs 0.0. **Decision: soil not adopted as default** (negligible gain outside western shrubland, map gaps on water/rock/built land, extra data dependency); kept as predictor set `soil`. Sub-kilometre skill needs predictors that genuinely vary at 240 m (topographic wetness, heat load, cold-air pooling, land cover for native ranges) |
| L11 | **The 240 m maps carry no measurable sub-kilometre information with WorldClim predictors** (revised 2026-10-04) | An 8-species western panel suggested a 240 m gain with occurrence training (+0.003 to +0.037 vs 18 km). Scaled to all 29 benchmark species with ≥ 20 BLM AIM presence plots (`scripts/resolution_study.py`, AUC inside the calibration area, medians): 240 m vs the same map at 0.96 km −0.0002 (occurrences) / −0.0004 (faithful); 240 m vs 18 km +0.0043 (positive for 21/29) / −0.0005 (13/29); occurrence-trained AUC peaks at 3.8–7.7 km. With ~1 km climate as input the 240 m product is an interpolated ~1 km map. The accuracy gain over Daru comes from occurrence training (AIM AUC 0.934 vs 0.846), not resolution | Real 240 m precision requires predictors that vary at 240 m (L10: soil, topographic wetness, heat load, cold-air pooling, land cover for native ranges). Caveat: AIM plots sample BLM land in the arid West |
| L12 | **Binary rule for the occurrence-trained product: P5 (5th-percentile training presence), not the ESS majority vote** | A native-range map must keep nearly all real populations inside it. ESS (equal training sensitivity and specificity) by construction drops ~20–25% of presences | **Measured 2026-10-04** (`scripts/threshold_study.py`, 28 AIM / 12 FIA species, medians): P5 sensitivity 0.96 (Daru 0.97–0.99) with TSS AIM 0.733 / FIA 0.795 over all plots vs ESS vote 0.580 / 0.653 and Daru's published binary 0.443 / 0.783; inside the area both cover, P5 0.587 / 0.484 vs Daru 0.308 / 0.250. P5 beats Daru's binary for 75–100% of species in every view. Adopted for the recommended product; mode `daru` keeps the ESS vote (faithful). Both binaries are stored in every range card |
| L13 | **Store the predictor source, not the warped stack**: the shared 240 m layer (20 bands, 21.0 GB float32) is a bilinear warp of WorldClim ~1 km, which covers the CONUS grid's extent in ~2.2 GB (~11× fewer cells); warp on the fly per tile/window on GPU, as range cards already do for the maps | The shared stack is now the dominant storage and distribution cost of the range-card design (cards: ~27 MB for 2,672 species × 2 models) | **Measured 2026-10-04**: lossless byte-shuffle + zstd of the warped stack gives only 2.2–2.3× (40 random 256² chunks × 20 bands), so compression alone cannot reach 10×; warping from source gives ~10× and ~20× with shuffle+zstd. A numpy bilinear warp with exact projection differs from the gdalwarp layers (approximate transformer, datum-operation choice) by up to 0.14 °C (bio1) and 33 m (elevation) on a Rocky Mountain window, so adopting it means redefining the canonical stack and re-recording every card's reference hashes: do it at the next full re-render. Warp command archived as `scripts/warp_conus_predictors.sh` |
| L14 | **Build the alpha hull from records thinned at Daru's predictor resolution (~9 km), not ~1 km** | Daru thins with `gridSample` at his predictor resolution before the hull; we thin at ~1 km for MaxEnt and cap the hull input at 20,000 points. Widespread species then feed rangeBuilder up to 20,000 points: Deschampsia cespitosa's Old World half takes ~25 min per attempt and hits degenerate rings (spherical and planar engines, jitter) that a sparser ~9 km set avoids. A ~9 km hull input is more faithful and roughly 30–100× fewer points for such species | changes the hull (hence `daru`-mode presences) of species already fitted, so apply at the next full re-run; hull fallbacks (`_robust_hull`) keep the current run complete in the meantime |
| L15 | **Record precision as the limit on map precision** | A model cannot learn 240 m structure from records located to ±1 km or worse; GBIF holds many such records (8-species panel: 54,002 of 118,711 records state an uncertainty ≤ 250 m, mostly GPS observations ≤ 30 m; many herbarium records state none). Filtering training records by stated uncertainty (or weighting by it) may be what the 250 m goal needs, more than finer predictors (L10, L11) | **Tested 2026-10-04, hypothesis not supported** (`panel_ablation.py --max-uncertainty 250`, 8 western species, BLM AIM, medians): records ≤ 250 m vs all records, climate predictors: AUC 0.799 vs 0.793, TSS (P5) 0.332 vs 0.277, 240 m vs 0.96 km −0.0002 vs −0.0002; with terrain predictors 0.801 / 0.335, 240 m vs 0.96 km −0.0029 (terrain at 240 m adds noise for these species). Precise records improve the binary maps somewhat but create no sub-kilometre skill. What limits 240 m skill is not record precision, nor soil or terrain at hand; candidates remaining: land cover / vegetation structure for realized native ranges, and plot-scale validation that can register 240 m differences (AIM plots are points; occupancy within a 240 m cell is set by microsites) |
| L16 | **Multi-scale predictors: the local 240 m value plus neighbourhood means (~1–5 km) of each predictor** | Positive control (`scripts/resolution_positive_control.py`, 8 western species, BLM AIM, gradient-boosted classifier on fine terrain, held-out AUC): terrain at 240 m alone predicts worse than its 0.96 km block average for 8/8 species (median −0.009), while adding the 240 m values to the 0.96 km context helps 7/8 (median +0.0012). Occupancy is set mostly by environmental context at ≥ 1 km, with a small but consistent local increment; the maps' AUC even peaks at 3.8–7.7 km aggregation (L11). Daru's single-scale predictors can capture neither | **Tested 2026-10-04** (8-species AIM panel): neighbourhood means (~7–8 km, `GlobalStack.focal`, `build_focal_stack.py`) as alternatives under VIF: AUC 0.802 vs 0.794 climate-only (5/8 better), TSS unchanged; decomposed (neighbourhood mean + local deviation, both kept by VIF, 17–18 predictors): AUC 0.777 on 6/8 species (mixed), TSS 0.309 vs 0.279; neither adds sub-kilometre skill. Small gain at most with MaxEnt's linear/hinge features; a model that learns cross-scale interactions may do better |
| L17 | **Which taxonomic concept a priority-list name means: GBIF's interpreted species vs the name a record was identified under** | Records are assigned by GBIF's interpreted species resolved through WCVP (closest to Daru). GBIF lumps some taxa WCVP keeps apart and iNaturalist splits others, so resolving each record's verbatim name instead changes the record set of 153 of 2,665 natives by > 5% (57 by > 25%): e.g. Pinus ponderosa loses 15,412 records identified as P. brachyptera and 7,405 as P. scopulorum (WCVP-accepted splits); Oxalis oregana's records split toward O. smalliana; Persea borbonia sends 1,403 to Nectandra hihua (a WCVP synonymy that looks wrong). For a plant in the horticultural trade the broad concept is usually meant ("ponderosa pine") | **Decided 2026-10-04 (horticultural judgement)**: keep GBIF's broader concept by default (a name on the priority list means the plant as sold); move a record to another species only when the name it was identified under (full name incl. variety/subspecies, resolved through WCVP) is itself a listed species. Splits toward unlisted taxa (Pinus brachyptera, P. scopulorum) are not applied; WCVP quirks toward unlisted names (Persea borbonia → Nectandra hihua) are ignored. `build_name_reassignment.py`, `run_fit --reassign` |
| L18 | **Neural range cards: store one shared feature field, not 18,600 maps** (design 2026-10-04, to be validated with the chosen joint model) | The DeepEarth joint model scores species s at cell x as f_s(x) = ⟨h(x), w_s⟩ + b_s. Every species' 240 m map is therefore a projection of one shared field h. The species vectors span at most d dimensions; with V the top-r right singular vectors of the species matrix W, f_s(x) = ⟨Vᵀh(x), Vᵀw_s⟩ + b_s exactly when r = rank(W), approximately for smaller r. Store: (i) the shared field Vᵀh at 240 m over CONUS land (~139M cells × r, int8 with per-channel scale; r = 32–48 → ~4.5–6.7 GB, plus averaged overviews for national/state zooms), (ii) per species r floats + bias + calibration ecoregion ids + thresholds (~0.3 kB), (iii) the 1.5 MB ecoregion raster. A 256² web tile decodes on a laptop CPU as one 65,536 × r by r matrix-vector product (~3M multiply-adds, ~5 ms in numpy); no GPU. The canonical map is defined as the decoded output, so storage is exact by construction (as L1). Expected 20–60× smaller than per-species 240 m rasters for 18,600 species (~150–280 GB as compressed uint8 GeoTIFFs) | **Measured 2026-10-04** (`native_ranges/neural_cards.py`, joint model free_v2, a 2,048² West window = 4.2M cells, 4 western species): factorizing the species-vector matrix alone is too lossy (64 dims = 59% of its energy, map rank correlation 0.67–0.96). The optimal rank-r factorization of the cells × species logit matrix (Eckart–Young; eigenvectors of the 2,661² species covariance over 200k sampled cells; field stored int8 with one scale per channel) gives map rank correlation with the full model 0.82–0.90 at r = 8, 0.95–0.97 at 16, **0.988–0.995 at 32** (RMS logit error 0.08–0.11 vs logit SD 0.6–0.95) and **0.997–0.999 at 64** (RMS 0.04–0.05). Single-threaded-equivalent numpy decode on this (loaded) CPU: 30–170 ms per million cells, i.e. ~2–11 ms per 256² tile. CONUS storage: shared field 4.4 GB (r = 32) or 8.9 GB (r = 64) + 192–320 bytes per species, vs ~150 GB of per-species compressed 240 m rasters for 18,600 species (17–34× smaller before entropy-coding the field). **Done 2026-10-05 (national, 18,595 species):** rank truncation is too lossy at this species count (rank 64 loses 0.011 AUC); the product uses transform coding of all 256 feature channels instead (metric-weighted KLT, one uniform step, int8/int16 tiles + zstd, `national_cards.py --codec klt`): 16.7 GB for CONUS + Alaska + Hawaii (CONUS field 13.3 GB), median AUC 0.9506 stored vs 0.9505 full model, 512² window decoded in 338 ms on CPU under full host load; ≈ 12× smaller than per-species zstd GeoTIFFs, 30× smaller than uncompressed clipped rasters (provenance 2026-10-05) |
| L18 | **Next predictor modalities for realized native ranges: land cover, canopy structure, surface water and wetlands, time-matched climate** | With WorldClim, SoilGrids and GLO-90 terrain the maps carry no measurable sub-kilometre skill (L10, L11, L15, L16). What delimits a realized range inside its climate envelope is habitat: forest vs open land, wetland vs upland, canopy height, human conversion. Records carry dates (mostly after 2005), so climate of the record's month and year can enter the spatio-temporal (Earth4D) channel instead of 1970–2000 normals | **Staged 2026-10-04** (`scripts/fetch_modalities.sh`, raw/modalities; all public, no account): NALCMS 2020 land cover 30 m (Canada, US, Mexico), ESA WorldCover 2021 10 m (Hawaii), Meta CHMv2 mean canopy height (~38 m overview of each North American tile), JRC surface-water occurrence 30 m, GLWD v2 wetlands, SoilGrids water retention 1 km, Global Aridity Index v3.1, Human Footprint 2000/2010/2020, TerraClimate monthly 1958–2025 (~4 km; 10 variables cut to 170°W–50°W, 5°N–75°N), Hawaii Climate Data Portal monthly rainfall and temperature 250 m. Next: aggregate each to the ~230 m training grid as class fractions / means and test on the AIM + VegBank + Hawaii panels with the resolution test (L11) **Land cover tested 2026-10-05 (NALCMS, 8 groups, 240 m fractions, benchmark, 2 seeds): not adopted** — VegBank test −0.0005, AIM +0.0003, FIA −0.0051 (FIA public coordinates are offset up to ~1.6 km; land cover at GBIF records also encodes where people record and plant), > 10 km +0.0034. A land-cover predictor would need an effort model at the same 240 m scale and exact plot coordinates to be judged fairly |
| L19 | **Date-matched background for time-varying predictors** | Presences have dates; MaxEnt's background points do not. Feeding record-month climate to presences but normals (or random dates) to the background would let a model separate them by date alone | Proposal: draw each background point's date from the dates of the species' own presences (or of all records in its calibration area, the target group), so presence and background share one date distribution; then only place differs. National tensors already carry presence year/month (`point_year`, `point_month`) |
| L20 | **Dated lycophyte placement** | The megatree has no lycophytes; the national tree attaches all 151 US lycophytes at the lycophyte crown (413 Ma, Morris et al. 2018), so they share nothing below it | A dated lycophyte phylogeny (e.g. from the fern-and-lycophyte timetrees) would give Isoetes, Selaginella and Lycopodiaceae their internal structure |
| L21 | **Fine-grid sampling of background points** | Background points are ~1 km cell centres, which fall exactly on corners of the ~230 m grid, so a point's ~230 m soil and terrain values come from whichever neighbour floating-point rounding selects (deterministic, but always one quadrant of the ~1 km cell). The priority-list (benchmark) joint tensors additionally sampled terrain at float32-rounded coordinates while soil used float64, so for 61% of background points terrain and soil came from different ~230 m cells (`add_fine.py`; the national tensors read both at the float64 coordinates) | Option: give each background point a seeded uniform position within its ~1 km cell (as the `soil` predictor set already does) or the mean of its 4 × 4 fine cells, so fine layers describe the cell rather than one corner |
| L22 | **Global soil layers for records outside North America** | The national joint model trains on each species' records across its whole WCVP native range (as Daru), so 6.8% of its 194M training points lie outside North America (Europe 3.5%, Asia 1.7%, South America 1.2%; 19% of them presences). The SoilGrids layers were fetched for North America only (−170…−50°, 5…75°N), so all of these points carry the soil-missing flag and the model learns soil preferences of widespread species from their North American records alone (inside North America 7.9% of points lack soil: water, urban, no data). Measured 2026-10-05 on a 3M-point sample of the national tensors | Proposal: sample SoilGrids 2.0 (global, ISRIC 1 km aggregated or 250 m COGs) at the out-of-domain points, refill columns 20–23 of the training cache, retrain the benchmark (2,661 species) and compare VegBank/AIM/FIA against the current model before any national retrain **Tested 2026-10-05:** overall change within seed noise (VegBank test +0.0008, AIM +0.0005, FIA −0.0003); species with ≥ 30% of presences abroad +0.0036 (60 species). Adopt in the next national model version |
